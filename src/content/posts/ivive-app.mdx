## Why it exists

Clearance sets how long a drug stays in the body, and so how large and how frequent a dose must be. Long before any human data exist, project teams estimate human clearance from a test-tube assay with liver microsomes or hepatocytes. Turning that in-vitro number into whole-body clearance is IVIVE: in-vitro to in-vivo extrapolation. ivive.app does that arithmetic with every intermediate shown.

## Validation, plainly

IVIVE from human liver microsomes and hepatocytes under-predicts in-vivo clearance by about 3-fold on average. The calculator does not hide that: every result carries the range in which observed plasma clearance fell for the middle half of 301 reference drugs, between 0.91× and 5.9× the well-stirred prediction.

On an independent Pfizer dataset (Tess et al. 2023), the 50% band held the observed clearance 58–68% of the time, never below its nominal coverage.

- The band assumes hepatic metabolism drives clearance. Where active uptake (OATP substrates such as statins or bosentan) or biliary excretion does, it under-predicts further, and the band does not cover that.
- Compounds with fu,p below 0.01 are flagged instead of given a range, because no range held for them.
- Multi-species allometry scored worst of the scaling methods measured, and the page says so.

## Use it from an LLM

ivive.app is also an MCP server, so Claude, ChatGPT or any MCP client can call the calculations instead of doing the arithmetic itself. A structure sent through an LLM also reaches that LLM's vendor; for proprietary compounds, check the vendor is cleared for them.

```sh
claude mcp add --transport http \
  ivive https://api.ivive.app/mcp/
```

Every calculation is also a plain HTTP endpoint; the reference is at <a href="https://api.ivive.app/docs">api.ivive.app/docs</a>.
