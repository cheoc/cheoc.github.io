## How we tested

A model is scored on samples it never saw during training. Which samples those are changes the score a lot, so we tested the same pipeline with five different settings. Every square is a sample.

## Datasets with a published result

15 properties. Only 6 come with the paper's own train/test split, so only those 6 compare like for like. The lower error in each row is bold. All numbers have 3 significant digits.

<PublishedTable />

<p class="note">Percentages in the calibration column are ensemble weights: each model's share of the final prediction. The pipeline picks them by greedily adding the model that most lowers the cross-validated error, 50 times, so the weights are multiples of 2% and add up to 100%. "Lower error" says how much lower the smaller error is than the larger one; "about equal" means within 1%.</p>

## Datasets without a published result

10 properties. Nobody has published an error for these, so we report ours on its own. Error: mean of 3 random splits. Calibration: model from the full pipeline, trained on all rows (run of 12 November). 3 significant digits.

## Under the hood

The pipeline is an API with a web interface on top. Everything runs in containers, on a CPU or with a GPU.

<dl class="stack">
  <div><dt>Modelling</dt><dd>Python 3.12, scikit-learn, NumPy, SciPy, Optuna for the search, LightGBM, PyTorch, TabPFN v2, SHAP for explanations</dd></div>
  <div><dt>API and data</dt><dd>FastAPI, Pydantic, SQLAlchemy, PostgreSQL 16, PDF reports with ReportLab and Matplotlib</dd></div>
