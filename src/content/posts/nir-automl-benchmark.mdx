## Why I tried this

At Evonik I spent more than three years around NIR: feedstuff analysis, prediction algorithms, two patents. Years later, at Eurofins, I built automated ML pipelines that take the hand work out of ADMET models. Putting the two together was the obvious next step. Can the AutoML approach build NIR calibrations?

The classic way is still a person at a desk: pretreat the spectra, look at a PCA, fit a PLS model, tune it, repeat. It works, and it takes an expert and a lot of time per property. I wanted to see how close a pipeline gets with one upload and a one-hour time limit.

<p class="note">Percentages in the calibration column are ensemble weights: each model's share of the final prediction. The pipeline picks them by greedily adding the model that most lowers the cross-validated error, 50 times, so the weights are multiples of 2% and add up to 100%. "Lower error" says how much lower the smaller error is than the larger one; "about equal" means within 1%.</p>

## I expected better

Honestly, I thought the pipeline would win more often. On the 6 properties where the comparison is fair, the published calibration has the lower error 5 times. Did I miss something? Maybe: a pretreatment the authors chose by eye, a wavelength range they cut, or simply years of knowing their samples. If you can see what it is, tell me.

What the pipeline does have is the rest of the job. It needs no chemometrics expert to run, it gives every property a validated model with a report in an hour, and it deploys as an API. For a lab with many properties and little time, being close is often enough.

## Can the published numbers be reproduced?

This is the bigger question. Only 6 of the 15 published results come with the paper's own train/test split. For the other 9 I cannot rerun what the authors did, so those rows stay indicative. A published error you cannot reproduce is hard to beat and hard to trust.

That is why I want more data. Spectra with reference values, ideally with the split that was used, make each comparison fairer. If you have some, the box at the end says how to send it.
