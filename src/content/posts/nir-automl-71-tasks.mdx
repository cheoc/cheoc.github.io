## Why a second run

In my [first NIR post](/posts/nir-automl-benchmark/) only 6 of 15 published results came with a published train/test split. The rest I could not reproduce, so most of that post compared the pipeline's numbers with results that could not be rerun.

[NIRBENCH-DL](https://github.com/DeepLightLab/NIRBENCH_DL_v0.1) fixes that ([live dashboard](https://deeplightlab.github.io/nirbench01/index.html)). It publishes 30 tasks with fixed train/test splits and the test errors of {NIRBENCH_MODELS} models on each: a tuned PLS, 13 deep-learning networks and two tabular foundation models, TabPFN 3.5 and TabICL v2. I ran 27 of the 30; tasks 4, 12 and 14 need a download from Eigenvector that I have not done yet.

I also ran 34 datasets from the [nirs4all dataset collection](https://github.com/GBeurier/nirs4all-datasets) and the 10 datasets of the first post again, on new splits. That makes 71 tasks, one measured property each, and 68 distinct dataset and property pairs: two NIRBENCH tasks repeat first-post data (see below), and NIRBENCH task 21 is task 15 with trimmed, SNV-treated spectra, so the 2021 pear data counts twice in the tally below.

## How I ran it

- Every task went through the full NIR AutoML pipeline, not a separate script: upload the spectra, ask for an automatic calibration, score the final model on the held-out test set.
- One hour per task covers the whole search, the outlier decision and the final fit. 26 of the 71 tasks stopped at that limit.
- The ensemble check changed since the first post. A mix of models is kept only if it beats the single best model by more than one standard error on rows held out from the search (outer folds); that check runs without TabPFN, which joins the mix afterwards with its own weight. Otherwise the single best model is used.
- The search runs on one PC with an Intel Core i7-4790K (4 cores, 8 threads) and an RTX 5060 Ti. Three model families (SVR, kernel ridge, random forest) are fitted on the GPU.
- The runs took place from 18 to 22 November 2026. The code changed in between, but only in GPU memory handling, SHAP and the upload size limit, not in how models are searched or chosen. 16 tasks I reran on the later code gave the same test error to 4 decimals on 15 of them; one moved by 1.4%.

## Runtime, and what to expect

Compute is where the NIR AutoML pipeline and NIRBENCH differ most. On the 27 NIRBENCH tasks, the median task trained for {Math.round(RUNTIME.oursMedianSeconds / 60)} minutes (shortest {Math.round(RUNTIME.oursMinSeconds)} s), {RUNTIME.oursTotalHours.toFixed(1)} hours for all 27, on the PC above. NIRBENCH reports wall times per model on an {RUNTIME.theirsHardware}: median {RUNTIME.theirsMedianSeconds.PLSRegression.toFixed(1)} s for PLS including its five-fold search, {RUNTIME.theirsMedianSeconds.TabPFN35.toFixed(1)} s for TabPFN 3.5, {RUNTIME.theirsMedianSeconds.TabICLv2.toFixed(1)} s for TabICL v2 and {Math.round(RUNTIME.theirsMedianSeconds.DeepSpectra)} s for DeepSpectra. The pipeline spends a few hundred times more compute per task than their strongest single models. That is the price of searching and combining many models instead of fitting one, and probably the main reason it can beat each of them; how much the search, the ensemble and TabPFN each add, I have not measured yet.

What to expect from it: small datasets finish in minutes, large ones use the whole hour. The gain over PLS is about the same at every size.

## NIRBENCH-DL: 27 tasks on the published splits

A gap counts only if it is larger than the noise of the test error. I use one standard error of an RMSE, 1/√(2 · test samples): about 14% for a test set of 27 samples, 2.5% for 800. With 27 comparisons, a few gaps beyond one standard error happen by chance, so I also show two. The formula assumes independent test rows. The mango tasks (22–25) hold several spectra per fruit, so their gaps are less certain than the chips show.

<TallyTable />

The fair comparison is the first row. NIRBENCH tunes its PLS by five-fold cross-validation on the training set, choosing preprocessing and 1–20 latent variables together, as a chemometrician would. "Best of {NIRBENCH_MODELS}" is the lowest test error among {NIRBENCH_MODELS} models per task, picked after seeing the test set, and NIRBENCH's deep-learning models run without hyperparameter tuning. So I do not read the second row as "beats deep learning"; it is a hard bar, not a rival.

NIRBENCH also ranks models by a consistency score: rank the models by test error within each dataset, map each rank to 0–100 (100 × (M − rank) / (M − 1), M models), and average over datasets. ## Where the NIR AutoML pipeline loses

- **Milk protein (15% higher than PLS).** NIRBENCH's PLS is the best model here. The pipeline's ensemble has a PLS member (30% of the weight) with 20 components, the cap of the search; more components gained less than 1% in cross-validation, so the cap is not the whole story. The search also stopped at the one-hour limit.
- **Mango S1 dry matter (6% higher than TabPFN 3.5).** The search stopped at the one-hour limit after 35 trials. On large tasks the time limit, not the method, sets how far the search gets.
- **Tomato set 4 (18% higher than TabPFN 3.5).** The test set has 27 samples, so 18% is about 1.3 standard errors: possibly noise.

## Read with care

- **The test sets are not fresh.** I scored these same NIRBENCH test sets on 14 November and changed the search afterwards. Tecator moisture, for example, went from 0.460 to 0.550. A result tuned against a reused test set is optimistic, even if no single change was aimed at it.
- **The pipeline flags its own weak models.** It marks a model unreliable when, on rows held out during training, the RPD is below 1.4, the slope of predicted against measured falls outside 0.8–1.2, or the error is more than 1.5 times the cross-validated error. The check does not see the benchmark test set. 11 of the 27 NIRBENCH models carry a flag, including 7 where the pipeline's error is below PLS beyond one standard error (3 beyond two). The flag is in the last column.
- **Foundation models are on both sides.** NIRBENCH runs two pretrained tabular models with frozen weights: TabPFN 3.5 and TabICL v2 (Qu et al. 2026). TabICL v2 is the best published model on 4 of the 27 tasks (3, 5, 19, 21). The NIR AutoML pipeline uses TabPFN v2 as one member of the ensemble; it carries weight in 40 of the 71 final models.

## The 10 datasets of the first post, on new splits

One random split per dataset (seed 42, stratified by the measured value) and one property each. These numbers do not replace the first post's: that post averaged 3 random splits and used the published splits where they existed. Corn protein, for example, is 0.051 here and 0.030 there; with 16 test samples, one split moves the error a lot. Two tasks appear twice in this post. Tecator moisture: 0.550 on NIRBENCH's published split (172 train / 43 test) and 0.512 here on the split used in this post (192 / 48). Wheat protein (handheld): NIRBENCH task 20 uses the same 403 samples, 0.364 on its split (323 / 80) and 0.363 here (322 / 81). Stratifying by the measured value also makes R² and RPD look better than a plain random split would.

## What the run says

On the one benchmark that publishes its splits, a one-hour AutoML run with no hand work has a lower error than a tuned PLS on 19 of 27 tasks beyond one standard error (12 beyond two, against 1 higher), and is lower than or within one standard error of the best of {NIRBENCH_MODELS} models on 24 of 27. On the harder nirs4all collection, the same pipeline produces weak models on half the datasets, and it says so.
